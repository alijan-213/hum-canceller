Hum Canceller

Listens to the noise, learns the exact shape of its repeating hum, then plays that shape inverted through your headphones. It cancels the steady, repeating part of a noise. Voices, traffic and random whoosh can't be predicted, so they stay.

How to tune it

  1. Learn the noise with your headphones off. Check the Cancellable figure. Above about 60% is a good sign. Below 30% means most of the noise is random and this won't help much.
  2. Put on wired headphones and press Start. Raise the level until the anti-wave is about as loud as the hum.
  3. Sweep Timing slowly. At one point the hum dips. Then go back and forth between Timing and Level for the deepest dip.
  4. If you hear a slow wobble or the hum creeps back, the pitch is slightly off. Nudge Pitch by 0.01 or 0.002 Hz until it holds still, then fix Timing again.
  5. Try fewer overtones if it sounds buzzy or the dip is shallow. Headphone cups block high overtones, so the fundamental alone often works best.
  6. Hold "Hold to compare" to hear the hum without the anti-wave.
  7. Switch to Live tracking once it works. The mic keeps listening while you wear the headphones, so the anti-wave follows the hum's pitch and loudness by itself. It also adds the device's audio delay for you. Keep the phone a little away from your headphones, sweep Timing once more, and leave it running.
  8. If Live tracking wobbles, set Tracking speed to Steady or tick "Hold current shape". That happens when the mic picks up the anti-wave from your headphones.

What to expect

  • The mic hears the room, not your ear, so it can learn the hum's shape and pitch but not its exact timing at your ear. That's why you tune Timing by ear.
  • Learned wave mode needs retuning when a mains hum drifts. Live tracking follows the drift itself.
  • Live tracking predicts the hum ahead to cover the delay, so it still only works on the repeating part. A real ANC headphone reacts to every sound within microseconds; a phone can't.
  • Stopping keeps the tuning. Reloading the page loses Timing.
  • Use wired headphones or earbuds. Phone speakers can't play low hums, and Bluetooth delay can drift.
  • On iPhone, turn off silent mode or you won't hear the anti-wave.
